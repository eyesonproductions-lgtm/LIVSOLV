What should I keep in my car in case I get stranded?

Build for the boring problems first: dead battery, low tire, no phone power, darkness, heat or cold, minor injury and waiting longer than expected for help. You do not need the most expensive version of every tool.

The short list: a way to deal with a dead battery, tire inflation, flashlight or headlamp, reflective warning gear, phone charging, water, shelf-stable snack, basic first aid, and seasonal protection.

Cheapest workable option: jumper cables

Traditional jumper cables do not contain a battery and do not need charging. The tradeoff is that you need another suitable vehicle and must connect them correctly.

Low tire

Budget/simple: 12V tire inflator

A 12V inflator that plugs into the car is attractive for an emergency kit because there is no separate inflator battery to remember to charge. Match the inflator to your vehicle and tire requirements.

The small renewable-power layer people forget

Solar + USB lantern: a compact lantern that can charge from USB quickly and from its own solar panel slowly gives you a second path to light if the car battery and your normal power bank are unavailable. Integrated solar is best treated as an endurance backup, not a promise of fast charging.

Rechargeable headlamp: choose USB rechargeable for normal use. If possible, keep a second light that accepts replaceable AA or AAA cells so one dead internal battery does not eliminate all your lighting.

Rechargeable AA/AAA cells: rather than buying questionable cells with tiny solar panels attached, use quality rechargeable cells and a USB smart charger. That charger can be powered from the vehicle, a power bank or a small solar panel.

Small folding solar panel: useful on longer trips or in remote areas for topping up a power bank, USB light, radio or battery charger. It is much more useful than expecting the tiny panel on a pocket power bank to refill a large battery quickly.

Solar/crank radio: if severe weather or remote travel is part of your risk profile, a NOAA-capable radio with USB plus solar or crank backup adds an information path that does not depend entirely on your phone.

LivSolv redundancy rule: whenever practical, small emergency electronics should have two ways to stay alive. USB is usually the fast everyday method. Solar, replaceable batteries or a hand crank is the independent backup.

Then add the cheap stuff

Visibility: reflective triangles or other warning gear.

Light: headlamp plus a second independent light source.

Phone: charging cable plus a dependable backup battery.

Waiting: water, shelf-stable snack and a basic first-aid kit.

Weather: blanket/layers in cold climates, and sun/heat protection where heat is the bigger risk.

If the car cannot move

Your kit should help you wait safely, not turn you into a roadside mechanic.

Make yourself visible where it is safe to do so, preserve phone battery, stay aware of traffic and weather, and call appropriate roadside or emergency help.
