What should I keep in my car in case I get stranded?

Build for the boring problems first: dead battery, flat tire, no phone power, darkness, cold or heat, minor injury and waiting longer than expected for help.

The short list: charged jump starter, tire inflator and pressure gauge, flashlight or headlamp, reflective warning gear, phone charging cable and backup battery, water, shelf-stable snack, basic first-aid kit, seasonal warmth or sun protection, and any essential personal medication you safely carry.

If the battery dies

Portable jump starter

This is one of the highest-value pieces of car emergency gear because it can solve a dead 12V battery without needing another vehicle. Choose one sized for your engine and keep it charged according to the manufacturer's instructions.

If a tire loses pressure

Compact inflator + gauge

A slow leak does not always require an immediate tow. An inflator can sometimes get you safely to a repair location, but a damaged tire or unsafe driving condition still requires proper roadside assistance.

If the car cannot move

Make yourself visible and buy time.

Use reflective warning equipment where it is safe to do so, preserve phone battery, stay aware of traffic and weather, and call appropriate roadside or emergency help. Your kit should help you wait safely, not convince you to attempt dangerous roadside repairs.

Build for your environment. A winter mountain car kit and a summer South Carolina car kit should not be identical. LiveSolv will break those out into simple seasonal versions rather than pretending one giant kit works everywhere.
