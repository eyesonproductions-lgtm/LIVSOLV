Emergency backup power

Gas, propane or solar: which generator should I buy?

Start with what you need to keep running. Then choose the power source. Here are the useful answers and actual options first. The generator-school stuff is farther down.

Fast answer: Gasoline usually gives you the most output. Propane stores better. Dual-fuel gives you two fuel choices, which is why it is our default recommendation for many households. A solar generator is really a battery power station plus solar panels: silent, indoor-safe and renewable, but slower to refill and much more limited by battery capacity and weather.
Gasoline

Best when maximum output matters

Usually the highest wattage from a dual-fuel machine and easy to obtain in normal conditions.

Catch: gasoline ages, needs safe storage and can become scarce during a widespread outage.

Propane

Best for stored emergency fuel

Portable cylinders store far better than gasoline and are easy to swap into a dual-fuel generator.

Catch: the same generator normally makes less power on propane.

Dual fuel

Best all-around combustion choice

You can use gasoline or propane depending on what is available. That redundancy matters more to us than arguing which fuel is theoretically cheapest.

Catch: it still makes carbon monoxide, still needs fuel and still lives outdoors while running.

Solar + battery

Best for quiet, repeatable small-to-medium power

No engine, no gasoline and no exhaust. Excellent for electronics and for stretching a larger outage system.

Catch: stored watt-hours are finite and cloudy weather can make solar refill painfully slow. Large appliance loads get expensive fast.

No fuel + no exhaust

Solar generator / power station

If your priorities are phones, Wi-Fi, laptops, medical electronics, lighting and refrigeration, a large LiFePO₄ power station with enough solar input may make more sense than running an engine continuously.

How long will it run?

Unlike a fuel generator, runtime is determined by battery watt-hours and your load. A 2kWh-class battery has roughly twice the stored energy of a 1kWh-class battery. Solar can replenish it, but only as fast as your panels, weather and battery input limit allow.

Downside: high-draw heating, central HVAC, well pumps and long cloudy outages can overwhelm a small battery system quickly. For multi-day resilience, we often prefer battery + solar + a fuel generator rather than pretending one technology solves everything.

Before any of the technical stuff, one rule matters more than all of it:

Every gasoline or propane generator produces carbon monoxide. Run it outdoors, at least 20 feet from the home and from doors, windows and vents, with exhaust directed away. Never run one in a garage, basement, crawlspace, camper or other enclosed or partly enclosed area. Use working battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarms indoors.

Now, the generator-school part

Running watts vs starting watts

Running watts are what equipment needs after it is operating. Motors and compressors can briefly need much more power when they start. That is why a refrigerator that averages modest power can still trip a generator that looks large enough on paper.

List the loads you truly need, check each device's running and startup demand, then leave margin rather than operating at the generator's absolute limit.

Gasoline vs propane in more detail

Gasoline normally produces the higher wattage rating. Propane stores far better and avoids stale gasoline sitting in a carburetor. Dual-fuel gives you both choices without requiring a giant residential propane tank.

Do not compare generators using only the biggest number printed on the box. Check the running and starting ratings on the fuel you actually expect to use.

How to make fuel last longer

Do not automatically run a fuel generator 24 hours a day. Many households can run it during higher-demand periods, cool the refrigerator/freezer, recharge battery power stations and devices, then shut it down during low-demand periods.

Use refrigerator/freezer thermometers rather than guessing. Food temperature, not a rigid generator schedule, determines whether cycling refrigeration is working.

Use the generator to charge batteries

A hybrid setup can be extremely effective. Let the generator handle large loads and rapidly recharge a battery station while it is already running. Then shut the noisy fuel-burning machine down and use the battery for quiet overnight loads such as Wi-Fi, phones, lights and electronics.

Connecting to the house

Never improvise a connection into household wiring. Powering home circuits requires appropriate transfer equipment and installation. Backfeeding through a receptacle can electrocute utility workers and other people.

Do these free things before buying anything

Walk the house: write down what actually stops working during an outage, including well pump, sump pump, furnace blower, refrigerator, freezer, medical equipment and internet.

Read the labels: record running watts and startup requirements for the loads you care about.

Decide what can take turns: you may not need to run the microwave, freezer and other heavy loads simultaneously.

Check what you already own: extension cords, propane cylinders, power banks and battery stations may change what generator size you need.

Practice: know where the generator will sit safely, how cords reach the loads and how to start and shut it down before an emergency.

The generator system is more than the generator

Plan the correct outdoor-rated cords or transfer equipment, fuel, engine oil, operating manual, safe weather protection, CO alarms and a written startup/shutdown procedure. Make sure more than one capable person in the household knows how it works.

Common failures are mundane: cords too short or underrated, missing adapters, stale fuel, dead starter batteries, a machine nobody can move, or only one person knowing how to operate it.

LivSolv rule: Bigger is not automatically better. A small efficient inverter generator can sip fuel while covering essentials. Buy the amount of generator your real loads justify, then use batteries and solar where they make the system quieter and more resilient.
