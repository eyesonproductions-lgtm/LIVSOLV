72-hour backup power without the math headache

How much backup power do I need for a 3-day outage?

For three days, the important question is not just “How big a battery should I buy?” It is “What do I need to keep running, and how will I put power back into the battery?” Here is the simple version.

The short answer

Food only: A roughly 2 kWh power station is a practical starting point, but three full days may require recharging depending on your refrigerator/freezer and conditions.

Food + Wi-Fi + lights: Start around 2 kWh and make a recharge plan part of the purchase.

More household essentials: Look at expandable systems rather than trying to make one portable battery do everything for 72 hours.

Pick the situation that sounds like your house

Keep the food cold

Refrigerator and/or freezer are the priority. Phones can be charged occasionally.

Start around 2 kWh

Food + Wi-Fi + lights

You also want the router, phones and a few efficient lights available.

2 kWh + a recharge plan

Keep life more normal

You want to add several household essentials and have more room for changing needs.

Choose expandable capacity

Why we are not promising “72 hours” from a particular battery: refrigerators and freezers cycle on and off, and their consumption changes with the appliance, room temperature, door openings and other conditions. A 72-hour outage also creates a different problem: eventually the battery itself needs energy.

The part people miss: recharging

Decide what absolutely has to stay on. Food refrigeration usually comes before convenience appliances.
Buy enough battery to get through the vulnerable periods. Bigger is useful, but endlessly buying battery capacity gets expensive and heavy.
Give yourself a way to refill it. Solar, a compatible generator, vehicle charging or restored grid power can turn a battery from a one-shot reserve into a system.

Solar ratings are maximums under suitable conditions, not guaranteed real-world output. Clouds, shade, panel angle, season and available daylight all matter.
