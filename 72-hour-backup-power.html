How much backup power do I need for a 3-day outage?

For three days, the important question is not just how big a battery to buy. It is what needs to stay running and how you will recharge the battery after the first day.

The short answer

Food only: a roughly 2 kWh power station is a practical starting point, but three full days may still require recharging depending on your refrigerator, freezer and conditions.

Food + Wi-Fi + lights: start around 2 kWh and include a recharge plan from the beginning.

More household essentials: look at expandable systems rather than expecting one portable battery to carry an entire house for 72 hours.

Why there is no honest universal “72-hour battery”: refrigerators and freezers cycle on and off, and their consumption changes with the appliance, room temperature, door openings and other conditions. By day two or three, recharging matters as much as battery size.

The part people miss: recharging

Decide what absolutely has to stay on. Food refrigeration usually comes before convenience appliances.
Buy enough battery to cover the vulnerable periods. Bigger capacity buys time, but it also gets expensive and heavy quickly.
Give yourself a way to refill it. Solar, a compatible generator, vehicle charging or restored grid power can turn a battery from a one-shot reserve into a repeatable system.

Solar ratings are maximums under suitable conditions, not guaranteed real-world output. Clouds, shade, panel angle, season and available daylight all matter.
