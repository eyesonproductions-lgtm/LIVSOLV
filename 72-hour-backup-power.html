Three days without overbuilding it

Build a 3-day backup power plan.

You do not need ten products and a spreadsheet. Pick the level of normal you want to preserve, buy one capable battery system, give it enough solar to actually refill, and know which loads deserve the power.

If you just want the answer

Most households: start around 2 kWh of battery capacity with roughly 800W of solar. That is a useful three-day foundation for refrigeration, phones, Wi-Fi, lights and selective laptop or appliance use.

Smaller budget / lighter loads: a 1 kWh system can work if you are disciplined and recharge it aggressively. Pair it with about 400W of solar rather than buying the battery alone.

Want more comfort or a path beyond three days? choose an expandable 2 kWh system. Add battery capacity and solar later instead of replacing the whole thing.

What does a 3-day plan actually look like?

Protect food

Refrigerator and freezer get first claim on the battery. Use thermometers and keep doors closed so you are powering temperature, not habit.

Keep communication alive

Phones, a router when service exists, radios and modest lighting use comparatively little energy. These are excellent battery loads.

Use big loads deliberately

A microwave, coffee maker or other high-watt appliance may be fine for a few minutes if the inverter supports it. Continuous electric heat is where stored energy disappears fast.

The important shift: Three-day backup is not about storing 72 hours of every watt you might use. It is about having enough stored energy to bridge nights and poor conditions, then replacing part of what you use each day.

If the sun does not cooperate

Live in the solution: Do not throw out solar because clouds exist. Give the system another refill path. A combustion generator can run outside for a shorter period to recharge the power station and handle heavy loads, then shut back down while the battery quietly carries the house essentials. Your vehicle can also provide a slower emergency charging route where supported. Solar handles the free sunny hours; fuel covers the bad-weather gap.

That is why we prefer a layered system over buying a massive battery and hoping it lasts exactly 72 hours.

Do these things first. They cost nothing.

Walk the house. Decide what actually matters if power is gone for three days: refrigerator, freezer, well pump, medical equipment, communications, lights, fans or heat-related equipment.
Separate must-run from nice-to-run. A freezer and phone charger are not competing with a space heater on equal terms.
Check appliance labels or measure them. Your real refrigerator, freezer and pump matter more than somebody else's internet estimate.
Practice for one day. Run your chosen loads from the power station, recharge from the actual panels, and see what percentage remains at bedtime. That tells you more than a generic runtime chart.

Why 800W of panels does not mean 800W all day

Panel wattage is a rated maximum. Clouds, shade, heat, season, panel angle and short winter days reduce actual production. That is why we size the solar side generously and still recommend another recharge path for serious outages.

Also stay within the power station's published solar voltage, current and wattage limits. More panels are useful only when they are electrically compatible with the station.
