Emergency water without turning your house into a warehouse

How much emergency water should I store at home?

Start with one gallon per person per day. Three days is the minimum practical target. If you have the room, working toward two weeks gives you much more breathing room.

The short answer

Minimum: 1 gallon per person, per day for 3 days.

Better: Build toward 7 days if storage space allows.

Best practical home goal: The CDC recommends trying for a 2-week supply when possible.

Pets, hot weather, pregnancy, illness and other needs can increase how much water you should keep.

That one-gallon rule is not just drinking water. It is meant to cover drinking plus basic cooking, brushing teeth and other essential uses. If you expect very hot weather, have pets, or want enough for more sanitation, store more.

Pick the storage style that fits your house

I need something easy to carry

Use several 5–7 gallon containers instead of one huge tank. You can move them, rotate them and spread the weight around.

Best for most homes

I have very little storage space

Stackable 3–4 gallon containers can fit under beds, on closet floors or in awkward spaces where a barrel will not.

Best for tight spaces

I want a serious shelter-in-place supply

Larger 18–32 gallon containers save space and reduce the number of individual cans, but they become extremely heavy when full.

One thing people underestimate: water is heavy

A gallon of water weighs a little over 8 pounds. That means a 7-gallon container is roughly 58 pounds of water before you count the container itself, and a 32-gallon tank holds more than 260 pounds of water.

That is why we generally prefer several manageable containers for ordinary households unless you already have a permanent place for a larger tank.

How long can you keep stored water?

The CDC says commercially bottled water is the safest and most reliable emergency source. If you fill your own food-grade containers with water, the CDC recommends replacing that stored water every six months.

Do not improvise with random containers. Use containers intended for food or drinking-water storage. Never use a container that previously held toxic chemicals.

What comes after stored water?

Stored water is your first layer. The next layer is having a safe way to treat or filter additional water if the outage lasts longer than your supply. We will cover that separately because a camping filter, boil-water advisory and chemically contaminated water are not the same problem.
