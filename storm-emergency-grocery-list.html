Storm tomorrow? Buy for the outage, not the apocalypse.

What should I get at the grocery store before a storm?

The goal is a few days of food and water that your household will actually use if the power goes out, plus enough cold-storage support to protect what is already in your refrigerator and freezer.

Fast list: drinking water, familiar no-cook food, a few quick-heat meals, shelf-stable protein, fruit, snacks, shelf-stable milk if your household uses it, ice or frozen water containers, coffee or comfort items, pet food, medications and any household essentials you are genuinely low on.

Start with what you already own

Before buying another cart of food, look at the pantry, refrigerator and freezer. Group what you already have into actual meals and decide what should be eaten first if the power fails.

If an outage is likely, freeze water containers with expansion room and freeze refrigerated foods you will not need immediately. More frozen mass helps a freezer hold temperature longer.

Protect the food you already paid for

Buy ice only if you have a plan for it. A hard cooler can protect the refrigerator foods you most care about if the outage lasts. Keep drinks in a separate cooler when possible so the food cooler stays closed.

Block ice or frozen water containers last longer than loose cubes. Frozen water also becomes usable water as it melts.

A refrigerator/freezer thermometer is more useful than guessing. Food safety decisions should be based on temperature, not smell or taste.

What not to waste money on

Do not buy weeks of dehydrated food if you have not solved water. Many emergency meals consume water and fuel.

Do not fill the cart with food nobody normally eats. A rotating pantry is more useful than a shelf of “survival” products your household hates.

Do not overbuy perishables immediately before a likely outage. You are creating another refrigeration problem.

If the power actually goes out

Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. Use refrigerated foods strategically, protect frozen foods, and move to shelf-stable meals as needed.

Safety sources

LivSolv uses current public-health guidance for food and water safety. During an outage, perishable food should be kept at 40°F or below, and questionable food should not be tasted to judge safety.
