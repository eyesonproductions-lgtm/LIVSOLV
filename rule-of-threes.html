A priority guide, not a countdown clock

The Rule of Threes, explained simply.

The traditional survival “Rule of Threes” is useful because it reminds you what tends to matter first in an emergency. It is not a set of guaranteed survival times and should never be treated like one.

Use this as a memory tool, not medical advice.

Real survival time varies enormously with temperature, health, exertion, injury, age, humidity, access to shade or shelter and many other conditions.

The traditional version

About 3 minutes

Without breathable air. Airway and breathing problems are immediate emergencies.

About 3 hours

Without adequate shelter in severe conditions. Exposure to heat, cold, wind or rain can become dangerous much faster than people expect.

About 3 days

Without water. The exact time varies, but safe water is a much higher priority than food.

About 3 weeks

Without food. This is the loosest part of the mnemonic and varies widely, but the point is simple: food usually comes after air, shelter and water.

What LiveSolv takes from it

We use the Rule of Threes to help organize priorities, not to scare people. In a normal home outage, your first concerns are usually not wilderness survival. They are things like safe temperature, safe water, medications and medical devices, lighting, communications and keeping food from spoiling.

Think in this order:

Immediate life safety → protection from dangerous heat or cold → safe water → communications and critical power → food → comfort and convenience.

Why shelter comes before food

The National Park Service identifies emergency shelter as one of the most important elements in a survival emergency because it protects against severe weather and exposure. That is why a preparedness plan should not start with giant food buckets while ignoring heat, cold, rain, shade and a safe place to stay.

What official emergency guidance looks like at home

The American Red Cross recommends keeping one gallon of water per person per day, with a 3-day evacuation supply and a 2-week home supply. It gives the same 3-day evacuation and 2-week home target for non-perishable food.

That is a much more useful household planning target than trying to calculate how long a person could theoretically survive without something.
