Portable solar

I just want to keep phones and small electronics charged.

You do not need a giant solar system for this job. Keep it simple: store the power in a good battery, then use a separate folding panel when you need to make more.

What we recommend: A USB-C power bank plus a separate 20–30W folding solar panel. Charge the battery from the wall before bad weather. During a longer outage, put the panel in the sun and recharge the battery from it. Then use the battery to charge your devices.

Add portable solar

A 20–30W folding USB solar panel

Aim for roughly 20–30W for phones and small electronics.

This is the piece that turns a regular power bank into a renewable outage setup. More panel surface gives you meaningfully more collection than the tiny panel attached to most all-in-one “solar power banks.”

The setup: Put the folding panel in clear sun and keep the battery shaded when the cable allows it. Recharge the power bank during daylight, then charge phones and other devices from the battery whenever you need them.

What about a power bank with the solar panel built into it?

We own and like the BLAVOR PN-W05 as a useful emergency gadget, and its built-in solar gives it another way to collect some energy. But a pocket-size solar surface cannot collect sunlight like a real folding panel. Buy an integrated solar bank because you like the complete gadget, not because you expect its tiny panel to replace a larger solar charger during a multi-day outage.

One thing to remember: the panel wants direct sun. The battery does not necessarily want to bake in it. A separate panel lets you position the solar surface in the sun while keeping the battery protected and within its specified operating temperature.

Need more than phones and small electronics?

Stop adding little panels and batteries. Once you are trying to run a refrigerator, freezer, several laptops or household appliances, a proper power station is the cleaner solution.
