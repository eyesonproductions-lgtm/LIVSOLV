How much emergency food should I actually keep at home?

Start with the solution. Keep food you can eat immediately, add a long-life no-water backup, then build deeper storage if you want it.

Fast answer: Start with 3 days, build toward 7, then 2 weeks or more. Do not make the whole reserve dependent on boiling water. Keep food you can open and eat.

How long can someone survive if food actually runs out?

A food reserve protects health, strength and judgment, but a preparedness target is not a biological countdown clock. LivSolv's founder has years of survival-television experience and has repeatedly watched people continue functioning for weeks on extremely little food. In one observed case, a person survived 34 days while consuming an estimated total of no more than about 800 calories over that period.

That is an extreme observation, not a safe target or fasting recommendation. Severe calorie deprivation drains energy and strength, consumes body reserves and tissue, impairs performance and can become medically dangerous. Individual vulnerability varies greatly.

The reason to know this is simple: if you are genuinely stranded and food is gone, do not assume you are about to die and give up. Keep solving the problems you can solve. Prioritize safe water, shelter and body temperature, conserve unnecessary exertion, signal for help and seek rescue.

Children, pregnant people, older adults, people with diabetes or other medical conditions, and people taking certain medications can become medically unstable much sooner.

How should I use the food already in my refrigerator and freezer?

An unopened refrigerator generally keeps food cold for about four hours. A full unopened freezer can hold temperature for roughly 48 hours, versus about 24 hours when half full. Keep the doors shut. Larger frozen water containers add thermal mass and become usable water as they melt. If using coolers, keeping drinks separate prevents constant opening of the cooler protecting perishables.

Keep perishable food at 40°F or below and never taste questionable food to decide whether it is safe.

Why not just buy three weeks of freeze-dried buckets?

Because dry emergency food can create a second emergency resource problem. It may require substantial preparation water and cooking fuel. A rotating pantry plus ready-to-eat meal bars gives you food that still works when those resources are limited. Long-storage meals then become an additional layer rather than the only plan.

I have $0 to spend. What can I do?

Inventory the pantry. Group shelf-stable food into actual meals and mark what can be eaten cold. Put no-cook food together so nobody has to improvise. Before predicted severe weather, freeze water containers and food you will not need immediately to add thermal mass to the freezer.
