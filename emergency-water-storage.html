Emergency water without turning your house into a warehouse

How much emergency water should I store at home?

Start with one gallon per person per day. Three days is the first target. If you have room, build toward two weeks. But if a storm is coming and you have no money for water containers, use the clean storage you already own before the tap stops working.

The short answer

Minimum target: 1 gallon per person per day for 3 days.

Better: build toward 7 days.

Strong home goal: CDC recommends trying for 2 weeks when possible.

Pets, hot weather, pregnancy, illness and other needs can increase the amount.

I have $0 to spend. What can I do before the water stops?

Fill clean containers you already own. Pitchers, reusable water bottles and durable food containers with tight lids can hold drinking water. Clean and sanitize them first. Do not use containers that held chemicals.

Use your freezer. Fill suitable containers with water while leaving expansion room, then freeze them. They become cold mass for the freezer and drinking water after they melt.

Yes, you can fill the bathtub, but treat open-tub water as utility water, not your primary drinking supply. A bare bathtub can contain soap residue, dirt and other contamination. Use that water for toilet flushing and cleaning. If you already own a food-safe bathtub bladder, that is different because the drinking water is sealed from the tub itself.

Remember water already inside the house. If the public supply becomes unavailable and the water was not contaminated, melted clean ice and water from a conventional domestic hot-water tank may be possible emergency sources. Accessing a water heater safely depends on the system, so follow the heater instructions and current public-health guidance before using it.

Do this before the emergency. Once officials say the water may be contaminated, do not fill drinking containers from that supply unless it is treated as directed.

Water is heavy

A gallon weighs a little over 8 pounds. A 7-gallon container holds roughly 58 pounds of water, and very large containers can become effectively immovable. Decide where a large container will live before filling it.

How long can you keep stored water?

CDC recommends commercially bottled water as a reliable emergency source. For water you fill yourself, use safe containers, clean and sanitize them properly, and replace stored water about every six months.

Do not improvise with chemical containers. A free container is not a bargain if it can contaminate your drinking water.

What comes after stored water?

Storage is the first layer. Treatment is the next. A camping filter, boil-water advisory and chemically contaminated water are different problems, so our treatment guide handles those separately.
