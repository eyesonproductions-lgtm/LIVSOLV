Quick Card 01
POWER OUTAGE: FIRST 15 MINUTES

Do these before you start improvising.

  1. Confirm whether the outage is only your home or a wider area.
  2. Check official utility/weather alerts while cellular service still works.
  3. Unplug sensitive electronics. Leave one light switched on so you know when power returns.
  4. Locate flashlights, batteries, power banks, medications and any powered medical-device backup.
  5. Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed.
  6. If using a generator, operate it outdoors and well away from doors, windows and vents. Never run it in a garage.
Decide early: Is this a stay-home outage, or is heat, cold, flooding, wildfire, medical dependency or another hazard creating a reason to leave?

Quick Card 02
FRIDGE + FREEZER: SAVE THE FOOD

The clock starts when the power stops.

  • Do not browse. Every door opening dumps cold air.
  • An unopened refrigerator generally keeps food cold for about 4 hours.
  • A full unopened freezer generally holds temperature about 48 hours; a half-full freezer about 24 hours.
  • Use a refrigerator/freezer thermometer when available. Perishable refrigerated food held above 40°F for more than 2 hours should generally be discarded.
  • Move food to a cooler with ice only when doing so will actually extend safe cold storage.
  • Never taste food to decide whether it is safe.
Priority: Eat/use the most perishable safe food first. Protect the freezer from unnecessary opening.

Quick Card 03
WATER OFF OR UNSAFE

Separate drinking water from everything else.

  1. If authorities issue a boil-water or do-not-drink notice, follow that notice first.
  2. Protect known-safe bottled/stored water for drinking, medications and food preparation.
  3. Do not assume clear water is safe after flooding, pressure loss or contamination warnings.
  4. Use non-potable water for toilet flushing or cleaning when appropriate. Keep it clearly separated from drinking water.
  5. If water is still flowing but loss is expected, fill clean food-grade containers before pressure is lost.
  6. Write down your remaining potable-water amount and ration deliberately, not randomly.
Baseline planning number: FEMA recommends storing at least 1 gallon per person per day for several days, with more needed for some people, pets and hot climates.

Quick Card 04
LEAVE NOW: 10-MINUTE GRAB

If the danger is increasing, possessions come second.

  1. People and pets.
  2. Medications, medical devices and critical mobility/accessibility equipment.
  3. Phones, chargers, power banks and keys.
  4. Wallets/IDs, essential documents or your prepared document pouch.
  5. Water and immediately usable food.
  6. Weather-appropriate clothing, sturdy shoes and basic hygiene items.
  7. Pet carrier/leash, food and medications.
  8. Leave using the safest official route. Do not wait for the car to be perfectly packed.

Quick Card 05
PHONES + COMMUNICATIONS DOWN

Stop everyone from making a different plan.

  1. Text before repeatedly calling when networks are congested.
  2. Switch off unnecessary cellular/background use and reduce screen brightness to preserve batteries.
  3. Use one agreed out-of-area contact as the household message relay.
  4. Check official emergency radio/NOAA Weather Radio or local broadcasts if internet service fails.
  5. Go to the pre-agreed meeting location if household members cannot communicate.
  6. Write important phone numbers on paper. A dead phone should not erase your contacts.

Quick Card 06
MEDICAL CONTINUITY

Protect the things that cannot simply wait.

  1. Identify medications or devices that cannot miss a dose, charge or temperature requirement.
  2. Keep medication labels/instructions with the medication.
  3. For refrigerated medicine, follow the medication manufacturer/pharmacist instructions rather than guessing.
  4. Preserve backup power for critical medical equipment before comfort electronics.
  5. If safe operation of essential equipment cannot be maintained, contact emergency services, your healthcare provider, utility medical-support program or relocate as appropriate.
