Blackout cooking

The power is out. What do I cook with?

You have several good options. Solar can cook without consuming fuel, gas stoves are fast and predictable, and wood-burning methods give you another fallback when stored fuel is limited.

Fast answer: For a family preparedness setup, we like one capable solar cooker plus one fuel-burning backup. Solar stretches your stored fuel whenever the sun cooperates. Propane, dual-fuel or butane handles nights, storms and times when you simply need faster cooking.

Before you cook: protect the cold food

Refrigerator

An unopened refrigerator generally keeps food cold for about four hours. After that, move priority perishables to ice or another safe cold-storage method.

Freezer

A full unopened freezer can hold temperature for about 48 hours, versus about 24 hours when half full.

Want the deeper explanation?

Why include solar if a gas stove is faster?

Because they solve different problems. Gas is faster and works regardless of sunlight, but every meal consumes stored fuel. Solar is weather-dependent, but its energy source replenishes itself. Using solar when conditions are good preserves propane or butane for darkness and bad weather.

Which fuel stove should I choose?

If price matters most, the Gas One GS-3400P gives you propane/butane flexibility for very little money. For family cooking, two burners are substantially more convenient, which is why the Coleman Triton is the middle choice. The Camp Chef Everest 2X is the performance upgrade. The Iwatani is here for people who specifically prefer a compact butane tabletop stove.

Solar cooking limitations

Solar cooking depends on adequate sunlight and usually takes longer than a gas burner. It is not the only cooking method we recommend. Treat it as one useful layer in a redundant cooking plan.

Carbon monoxide and combustion safety

Fuel-burning equipment can produce carbon monoxide. Never use generators, charcoal grills, rocket stoves or outdoor-only camp stoves in enclosed or partially enclosed spaces. Follow the current manual for any appliance whose manufacturer specifically permits indoor operation, including all ventilation and clearance requirements, and keep working CO alarms in the home.
