Home + family

Keep the people okay when the systems are not.

Start with what you can do for free. Add gear only where it solves a real problem.

Do these free things first.

Pick one place everyone will meet if phones fail. Put emergency numbers and medications on paper. Put the flashlights, batteries and first-aid supplies you already own in places everyone can find. Decide which room is safest in extreme heat or cold.

Why these four things first?

They solve the most common failure points without buying anything: separated family members, dead phones, supplies nobody can find and trying to manage an entire house instead of protecting the people in it.

One important solar-charging note

The built-in solar panel is small. Use USB to fully charge the flashlight before an emergency and treat the solar panel as a way to replenish power over time when the grid is unavailable, not as a fast charger.

How long does solar charging take?

LuminAID lists roughly 16–20 hours of direct outdoor sunlight for the PackLite Max. Think of the built-in panel as a way to keep replenishing light during a long outage, not as a fast phone-charging solar panel.
