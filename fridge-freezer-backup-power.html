What size power station do I need to keep my refrigerator and freezer running?

Start with what you actually need to keep cold and how long you want it covered. Then plan how you will replenish the energy if the outage lasts longer than expected.

The short answer

For one refrigerator during a shorter outage, start around the 1 kWh class. For a refrigerator plus separate freezer, internet and lights, the 2 kWh class gives more breathing room. For multi-day outages, a recharge plan is as important as battery capacity.

What people discover in real outages

LivSolv cross-checks technical guidance against firsthand outage reports and experienced preparedness communities. A few lessons recur:

Do not automatically power the refrigerator the instant the grid drops. Keeping the doors closed preserves stored cold and saves battery or fuel for later. A refrigerator/freezer thermometer helps you make that decision using temperature instead of anxiety.

A power outage can become a water outage. Homes on electric well pumps can lose running water too, so refrigeration power should not consume every watt of your backup plan.

Cell and data service can degrade. Towers may lose power or become overloaded. Protect some stored energy for communications instead of spending everything on comfort loads.

Test the actual system before the emergency. People repeatedly discover that a UPS, extension-cord arrangement, solar array or battery that looked adequate on paper cannot run the intended load. Plug in the real refrigerator and freezer and learn the system while the grid still works.

Redundancy beats one enormous battery. Battery power is quiet and useful indoors. Solar can extend it when conditions cooperate. A properly operated generator can refill batteries or carry larger loads. Different tools cover each other's weaknesses.

How to make sure before you buy

Find the EnergyGuide label. Use the refrigerator's estimated annual kWh as a starting point.
Measure the real load if possible. A plug-in energy meter over a normal day gives much better planning data than a generic refrigerator estimate.
Add every cold appliance you intend to protect. A separate freezer changes the calculation.
Test startup and runtime. Confirm the station can start the compressor and observe how quickly its state of charge falls.
Test your recharge path too. Do not wait for an outage to discover your solar input, generator charger or cable arrangement does not work as expected.

Use energy in priority order

For most households, refrigeration, essential communications, necessary medical loads and efficient lighting deserve power before high-draw comfort appliances. Your priorities may differ, but decide them before the outage rather than while the battery percentage is falling.
