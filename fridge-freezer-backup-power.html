Which one should I buy?

One refrigerator, shorter outage: the 1 kWh class is the cheaper, lighter place to start.

Refrigerator + freezer or longer outage: start around 2 kWh and make recharging part of the purchase.

Several days: stored capacity alone is not the solution. Solar, generator charging or another refill route becomes central.

Why the runtime range is so wide

A refrigerator does not draw its compressor wattage continuously. It cycles. Temperature, age, size, defrost cycles, ice makers and door openings change consumption. Check the yellow EnergyGuide label. Annual kWh ÷ 365 gives estimated daily kWh. Measuring the appliance for a normal day is better still.

Capacity and output answer different questions. Watt-hours tell you roughly how long the battery can supply energy. AC output and surge capability determine whether the station can successfully start and operate the compressor.

What real outages teach

Do not spend battery immediately just because the grid dropped. Closed refrigerator and freezer doors preserve cold. Use appliance thermometers and food-safety limits rather than anxiety.

Protect energy for more than food. Well pumps, communications and medical loads may also depend on electricity.

Test the real system. Plug in your actual refrigerator and freezer, confirm compressor startup, observe battery drain, then test the solar or generator recharge path too.

Redundancy wins. Battery power is quiet and indoor-safe. Solar can replenish it when weather cooperates. A correctly operated generator can carry heavier loads or refill batteries when sunlight is poor.

Simple runtime math

Use your appliance's average energy consumption, not just its compressor running watts. A practical first estimate is usable battery watt-hours divided by the appliance's average watts over time. Allow for inverter and system losses. For a more accurate household answer, measure your refrigerator for 24 hours.

Do not treat any generic refrigerator runtime as a promise. The estimates above deliberately use ranges because real refrigerators vary too much for a single honest number.
