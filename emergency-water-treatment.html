How do I make questionable water safer to drink?

First choose the cleanest available source. Then use a treatment that actually matches the risk. Here are the useful options before we get into the details.

If you can boil it: For suspected germs, boiling clear water is the strongest simple household fallback. Bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet. If you suspect fuel, pesticides, toxic chemicals or radioactive contamination, do not try to rescue that water with a filter or boiling. Find another source.

The simple treatment sequence

1. Pick the cleanest source

Avoid floodwater, oily sheen, chemical odors, obvious sewage and water near industrial or agricultural contamination whenever another source exists.

2. Clear out the crud

Let muddy water settle, then pour off the clearer water. A clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter can remove more visible debris. This step does not kill germs.

3. Treat the germs

Boil, use a filter or purifier that matches the hazard, or use an appropriate disinfectant exactly according to its directions.

4. Keep clean water clean

Put treated water into a clean container and keep the dirty side of filters, hoses and vessels away from the treated water.

Filter and purifier are not the same thing

Filter

A filter with an absolute pore size of 0.3 micron or smaller can remove bacteria and parasites, but common portable filters generally do not remove viruses.

Purifier

A verified purifier can provide broader biological protection. Check the actual manufacturer's claims because the word “purifier” should not be assumed from appearance or price.

What boiling solves

For biological contamination, boiling is simple and extremely useful: a rolling boil for 1 minute kills viruses, bacteria and parasites. Above 6,500 feet, boil for 3 minutes.

If the water is cloudy, let it settle and pre-filter visible material first. If chemical contamination is suspected, boiling can concentrate some contaminants rather than solve the problem. Use another water source.

Do not trust a seep beside a creek just because it looks clear

Soil can remove visible sediment, but water from a shallow hand-dug seep should still be treated as untreated natural water. Clear does not mean microbiologically safe.

I have $0 to spend. What can I use at home?

If the concern is germs rather than chemicals, a pot, safe heat source and clean storage container may already give you the strongest simple fallback: boiling. Cloudy water can first settle and be passed through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter before boiling. That pre-filter removes debris, not pathogens.

A few things worth knowing

Freezing can damage some hollow-fiber filters. Follow the manufacturer's storage instructions.

Family throughput matters. A straw is excellent for one person. Processing gallons every day is why gravity systems become much more practical at home.

No single treatment fixes everything. Filters clog, chemicals run out and boiling consumes fuel. Having two workable methods is much more useful than buying five nearly identical filters.
