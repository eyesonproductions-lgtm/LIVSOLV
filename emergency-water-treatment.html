Emergency water treatment without pretending every filter does the same thing

How do I make questionable water safe to drink?

First figure out what kind of problem you may have. Boiling, disinfecting, filtering and purifying are not interchangeable, and none of them can fix every kind of contaminated water.

The short answer

Known or suspected germs: boiling is the most dependable household method.

Backcountry-style water: a filter can remove bacteria and parasites, but many ordinary filters do not remove viruses.

When viruses are also a concern: use a true purifier rated for viruses, bacteria and protozoa.

Fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive contamination: do not try to fix it with boiling or a normal emergency filter. Use a different water source.

Use the method that matches the problem

Situation Good first choice Important limitation
Boil-water advisory or suspected germs Boil clear water at a rolling boil for 1 minute Above 6,500 ft, CDC says 3 minutes
Stream, lake or questionable fresh water Filter or purifier designed for microbiological hazards Check whether it removes viruses, not just bacteria and parasites
No heat available Use an appropriate disinfectant or purifier Chemical disinfection can be less effective against some parasites
Fuel or toxic-chemical contamination Use bottled water or another safe source Boiling and ordinary disinfection do not make it safe
Do not use taste, smell or clarity as proof that water is safe. Dangerous microorganisms can be present in water that looks perfectly clear.

If you can boil it, boil it

The CDC recommends bringing clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute. At elevations above 6,500 feet, boil for 3 minutes. If the water is cloudy, let sediment settle or pre-filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter first.

Let the boiled water cool and store it in clean, sanitized containers with tight-fitting covers.

Filter versus purifier: this is the distinction that matters

Basic filter

Usually intended for bacteria, protozoa, sediment and often microplastics.

Good for many outdoor water sources

Virus-rated purifier

Adds protection against viruses, which matters in some disaster, travel and human-contamination scenarios.

Broader biological protection

Chemical problem

Some activated-carbon systems can reduce certain chemicals, tastes and odors, but that does not mean they can make severely contaminated water safe.

What about bleach or purification tablets?

CDC guidance allows chemical disinfection when boiling is not possible, but the exact dose depends on the disinfectant and its concentration. Household bleach products vary, so we are not going to publish a one-size-fits-all number that could be wrong for the bottle in your house.

Follow current CDC instructions or the product label for disinfecting drinking water. Chemical disinfectants may also be less effective than boiling against some parasites such as Cryptosporidium and Giardia.

What we would keep at home

For an ordinary household, we would layer the plan: stored drinking water first, a way to boil water second, and a family-scale purifier as backup if safe tap water is unavailable for longer than expected. A small portable filter or purifier is useful as an additional evacuation or vehicle option.

Water preparedness works best in layers. Stored water handles the first days. Treatment equipment gives you options if the disruption lasts longer. Neither replaces the other.
