Home + Family · Outage security

Keep watch when the power and Wi-Fi are down.

A blackout should not take your exterior lighting, entry alarms and property monitoring with it. Build several inexpensive layers that use sunlight, batteries and cellular service instead of depending on the same grid and router that may fail.

The LivSolv approach: light the approaches, create local alarms at doors and windows, add early warning farther from the house, and use a cellular solar camera where seeing what happened actually matters.

Build four simple security rings

Ring 1 · Property edge: driveway alert and, where useful, a cellular trail camera at the road, gate or long approach.

Ring 2 · House perimeter: solar motion floodlights covering dark sides, rear approaches, garages and yards.

Ring 3 · Entry points: solar lighting at doors and steps plus battery-operated magnetic door/window alarms.

Ring 4 · High-value equipment: generator, fuel, solar equipment, shed, detached garage, RV or other outage-critical gear gets its own light, lock and camera where appropriate.

6. Physical security still works with zero watts

Doors: use functioning deadbolts and make sure frames and strike plates are secure.

Windows: repair broken locks; appropriate dowels or stops can add a simple physical barrier on compatible sliding windows and doors.

Garage: know how the manual release works and how the door can be secured when an automatic opener is unavailable.

Generators and fuel: place generators outdoors at a safe distance for carbon-monoxide protection, but secure valuable equipment against easy removal without creating a fire, ventilation or trip hazard.

Gates, sheds and RVs: inspect locks before storm season rather than discovering a failed latch during an outage.

A sensible budget build

Start almost free: test locks, trim sight-blocking vegetation where appropriate, move existing battery lights where they help, and decide which approaches matter.

Add inexpensive layers: a multipack of solar motion lights plus battery door/window alarms.

Add early warning: a driveway sensor if your home sits back from the road.

Add one camera last: put a cellular solar camera at the single location where a picture on your phone gives you the most useful information.

What should I do before a storm?

Charge rechargeable security devices, confirm camera and cellular plans are active, clean solar panels, replace weak alarm batteries, walk-test motion zones after dark, verify local storage is installed where supported, and make sure every household member knows which alarms and lights are expected to activate.

Should I leave every exterior light on all night?

Not necessarily. A layered plan can use modest dusk-to-dawn path or entry lighting with brighter motion-activated security lighting at approaches. The goal is useful visibility and warning, not turning the property into a stadium.

No bunker required. The useful system is the one your household can afford, install and test. A few independent layers are more resilient than one expensive security system that shares a single point of failure.
