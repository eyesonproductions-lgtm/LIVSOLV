Power + light

Small devices that quietly replace several other things in your emergency kit.

Your phone charger, wireless charging pad, emergency flashlight and last-ditch solar backup are all in one rugged little battery.

Its 10,000mAh battery can recharge devices by cable or wireless charging, while the built-in lights mean the battery itself can become emergency illumination. The integrated solar panel gives you another way to put some energy back in when an outlet is not available.

What that means in real life: charge it from the wall and keep it ready. If the grid goes down, you already have portable phone power and light in one device. The little solar panel is slow emergency backup, not a substitute for a real solar charger.

Communication when your phone stops being a phone

These are the gadgets that matter when the little bars at the top of your phone disappear.

OFF-GRID MESSAGING

ZOLEO satellite communicator

Your phone can have zero cell service and zero Wi-Fi and you can still send and receive messages through satellites.

Pair ZOLEO with your phone and use the familiar app to send two-way app messages, SMS texts and email. When cellular and Wi-Fi are available it can use them. When they disappear, an active service plan lets ZOLEO move supported messages through the Iridium satellite network instead.

What that means in real life: you can be deep in the mountains with no bars, stranded far from a tower, or sitting in an area where a major outage has knocked out your local cellular and internet connection and still have a path to tell family, “I’m okay,” “I’m stuck,” or “Here is where I am,” as long as the device has the required satellite view and the receiving side still has a functioning way to receive the message.

Check-in: press a dedicated button and ZOLEO can tell chosen contacts you are okay and optionally include your GPS coordinates. That button works even if your paired phone is dead or damaged.

SOS: press the protected SOS button and the device can send your GPS position to a 24/7 emergency response partner. With the app, you can exchange two-way messages so responders know whether this is an injury, vehicle problem, medical emergency or something else instead of receiving only a mystery distress signal.

And it keeps going: GPS/GLONASS location, location sharing, weather forecasts, a dedicated SMS number and email, 200+ hours of standby battery under stated conditions, IP68 dust/water resistance, shock resistance and USB-C charging on the current ZL1500.

Buy ZOLEO →

A tiny rechargeable radio that can receive NOAA weather information, be programmed from a phone and do far more than a basic walkie-talkie once you actually learn how to use it.

It offers USB-C charging, Bluetooth phone programming, repeater capability and broad receive coverage. That gives it a place in a communications plan that does not depend entirely on cell towers.

Medical tools worth knowing about

The gadgets here earn their spot because one compact object can do several very specific jobs.

SIX TOOLS IN ONE

Leatherman Raptor Rescue

Trauma shears, seatbelt/strap cutter, ring cutter, oxygen-tank wrench, ruler and carbide glass breaker that fold down to about five inches.

The shears lock open for cutting clothing and other material, then fold for compact carry. The strap cutter can get through a belt or webbing, the ring cutter is designed for soft-metal rings, and the carbide breaker gives the tool an emergency-extraction job too.

What that means in real life: instead of stuffing several specialty tools into a car or medical bag, one compact tool can cut clothing away from an injury, cut a trapped strap, remove certain rings as swelling develops, help access an oxygen cylinder and provide a glass-breaking tool.
See Raptor Rescue →
FLAT UNTIL YOU NEED IT

SAM Splint

It starts as a light, flexible strip and becomes a surprisingly rigid support when you curve and shape it around an injury.

The aluminum-core splint is lightweight, reusable, cleanable and radiolucent. It can be shaped for different extremities rather than requiring you to carry a separate rigid splint for every possible injury.

What that means in real life: an arm, wrist, ankle or other extremity can be immobilized with something that takes very little room in a medical kit while you arrange proper care.

Adjustable zip-style wound closures

Think butterfly closures with tiny adjustable zip ties that let you gradually draw the skin edges toward each other without putting adhesive directly across the wound.
