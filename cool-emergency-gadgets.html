COOL + ACTUALLY USEFUL

Emergency gear that earns its space.

No tactical junk drawer. These are the products that make you stop and say, “Wait, it does all of that?” Every feature needs to solve a real problem.

Some of these are things we personally own. Others are products we researched because they solve a problem unusually well. We label the difference. The point is not to collect gadgets. It is to find the rare gadget that replaces three ordinary things, works when normal systems fail, or solves a problem in a surprisingly smart way.

Power + light that does more than one job

The best emergency electronics are useful on an ordinary Tuesday too. That is how you know they will be charged, familiar and actually nearby when something goes wrong.

OWNED + LOVED

BLAVOR PN-W05 solar power bank

It is a 10,000mAh battery, cable charger, wireless phone charger, emergency light and backup solar charger in one little brick.

Charge it normally before a storm and use the small solar panel as the backup-to-the-backup rather than expecting a pocket panel to refill 10,000mAh quickly. The built-in lights mean one charged object can keep a phone alive and provide light.

Why it earns space: fewer separate cords, lights and batteries to remember.

FLOATING LIGHT + POWER

LuminAID PackLite Max 2-in-1

It is a room lantern, solar light, phone charger and a waterproof light that actually floats.

The inflatable lantern packs nearly flat, then opens into a broad-area light. LuminAID rates it for up to about 50 hours of light, and the 2,000mAh internal battery can top up a phone, camera, headlamp or other small USB device. Leave it in the sun to recharge without grid power.

Why the floating part matters: floods, wet campsites, boats, leaking roofs and kids moving through a dark house with a light that does not become useless when it hits water.

OWNED + LOVED

E-SHIDAI solar rechargeable flashlight

A flashlight you can deliberately leave sitting in a sunny window so there is always a light quietly collecting backup power.

That changes the emergency-light problem from “where are the batteries?” to “grab the light.” Solar is not the fastest charging method, but for a device that spends most of its life waiting, passive top-ups are exactly the point.

Why we like it: it is boringly easy to keep ready.

OWNED + LOVED

VENTY Original folding fan

This one is ridiculous in the best way: fan, telescoping floor fan, table fan, oscillating fan, LED light, phone power bank and remote-controlled bedside fan.

It has four fan speeds, 120° oscillation and 180° rotation. The stem extends so it can sit low on a table or rise into a floor fan. The base has three levels of ambient LED light, it is cordless, and VENTY rates runtime up to 48 hours depending on settings. The remote works from up to about 30 feet away.

Then there is the feature that makes it emergency gear: the battery can send power back out through USB-C to charge your phone.

Why it earns space: during a summer outage, moving air can be the difference between miserable and manageable. At night it becomes the room light. When your phone is dying, it becomes another battery bank.

Communication when the phone stops being a phone

A charged smartphone is not independent communication. These give you another path when towers, Wi-Fi or normal infrastructure are missing.

SATELLITE TEXT + SOS + GPS

ZOLEO satellite communicator

Your phone can say “No Service” and you can still send a text to someone you love.

Pair ZOLEO with the app and, with an active plan and a reasonably clear view of the sky, messages can route over the Iridium satellite network when cellular and Wi-Fi are unavailable. You can send and receive messages, share GPS location and get weather information.

Its dedicated Check-In button lets you send a simple “I’m okay” message with location. The physical SOS button can connect you to a monitored emergency-response service and transmit your location.

The really important part: SOS and Check-In can work from the ZOLEO device itself. If your phone is dead, smashed or soaked, you have not necessarily lost your last way to call for help.

Satellite service requires an active ZOLEO subscription. Device versions differ, including connector type, so check the exact model before buying.

See ZOLEO →
OWNED · NOT FIELD-TESTED YET

TIDRADIO TD-H3

A compact handheld radio that gives you a communication path that is not a cell tower.

Useful capability depends on how it is programmed, which frequencies you use and the applicable licensing rules. The smart move is to learn it before the emergency instead of owning a very capable mystery brick.

Why it is here: radios become dramatically more useful when your household already knows the channel, plan and limitations.
IPX6 + NOAA + POWER

Raddy SL10 emergency radio

This is the emergency radio that finally checks the weather-resistance box too: NOAA weather radio, AM/FM, a 360° lantern, flashlight, Bluetooth speaker, SOS/strobe and a 10,000mAh phone power bank in one IPX6-rated unit.

It receives all seven NOAA weather-band frequencies plus AM/FM, so it can pull official weather information without cell service. The 360° lantern has three light modes, while the separate flashlight gives you a directional beam. Red/blue SOS lighting adds a visible distress signal.

The 10,000mAh battery can charge a phone through USB output. You can recharge the radio by USB-C, its solar panel or hand crank, giving you multiple ways to keep it alive when outlets are unavailable.

Why this replaces the CR1009 here: our CR1009 has a lot of clever features, but it is not a radio we would deliberately expose to rain. The SL10 is rated IPX6, so it is a much better fit for an emergency-radio recommendation that may actually get used outdoors in bad weather.

Water resistant does not mean submersible. Keep ports closed and do not intentionally dunk it.

Medical gear that makes you say, “Why isn’t that in every kit?”

SHEARS + CUTTERS + MORE

Leatherman Raptor Rescue

Folding medical shears with a strap cutter, ring cutter, oxygen-tank wrench and carbide glass breaker built into the same tool.

The shears fold down and lock into a compact package, and the holster keeps them accessible. The ruler printed along the shear blade adds another surprisingly practical feature.

Why it earns space: cutting clothing, straps, stubborn material or a ring from a swelling finger are very different jobs. This handles several without turning the first-aid kit into a toolbox.
MOLDABLE SPLINT

SAM Splint

It stores nearly flat and floppy, then becomes a surprisingly rigid support once you bend it into the right shape.

It can be cut, folded and formed around different injuries, then cleaned and reused. It is lightweight and radiolucent, so it does not have to be removed for X-rays.

Why it is clever: one compact sheet can become a wrist, arm, ankle, finger or improvised cervical support instead of carrying multiple rigid splints.
OWNED + LOVED

Adjustable zip-style wound closures

Adhesive strips sit on either side of a small cut and adjustable plastic ties pull the edges together without a needle.

That makes them an interesting addition for appropriate minor cuts when ordinary adhesive strips are not holding the edges together well.

Not for deep, contaminated, heavily bleeding, gaping, bite or puncture wounds, or wounds involving deeper structures. Those need professional medical evaluation.
