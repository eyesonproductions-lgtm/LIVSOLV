How prepared do you actually need to be?

Preparedness is not just storm preparation. Electricity, water, communications, transportation or normal services can fail for many reasons. Start with the disruption that would hurt your household most, cover the basics, then add redundancy where it genuinely helps.

Level 1

Need it now

Power may be going out, an emergency is developing, normal services are already disrupted, or you have only a day or two to get ready. Focus on water, food, medications, lighting, charging, reliable information and free steps you can take immediately.

Level 2

Get through a few days

Cover water, food, safe cooking, first aid, communications, temperature control and enough backup power for the loads that truly matter.

Level 3

Be comfortably prepared

Add more margin: longer food and water reserves, dependable recharging, better communications, generator or power-station capability where useful, and household-specific emergency plans.

Level 4

Build serious resilience

Add longer-duration energy, meaningful solar recharging, deeper water and food reserves, fuel redundancy, evacuation planning and multiple ways to communicate when normal systems fail.

Think in failures, not disaster labels.
A hurricane, tornado, wildfire, ice storm, heat wave, equipment failure, grid emergency or planned shutoff can create many of the same household problems: no electricity, unsafe temperatures, spoiled food, limited water, lost communications and difficulty getting supplies. Build capabilities that work across causes.
Preparedness starts with capabilities, not shopping.
If you already own something that safely solves the problem, use it. Spend money on actual gaps, not on building an impressive pile of emergency gear.
