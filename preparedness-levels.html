Prepare ahead

Build resilience without buying everything.

Start with the failures that would hurt your household most. Cover the basics. Test them. Then add redundancy only where it earns its place.

01

Cover the first day

Safe light, drinking water, medications, phone power, food protection and reliable information. Much of this layer may already be in your house.

02

Get through a few days

Add safe cooking, first aid, communications, temperature planning and enough backup power for the loads that genuinely matter.

03

Add breathing room

Increase food and water reserves, add dependable recharging, strengthen communications and build household-specific plans.

04

Build real redundancy

Longer-duration energy, meaningful solar recharging, fuel options, evacuation planning and multiple ways to communicate when normal systems fail.

Think in failures, not disaster labels.

A hurricane, tornado, wildfire, ice storm, heat wave, equipment failure, grid emergency or planned shutoff can produce the same household problems: no electricity, unsafe temperatures, spoiled food, limited water and lost communications. Build capabilities that work across causes.

Test the system you think you have.

A battery specification, generator wattage or box of emergency food is not the same as a working household plan. Run the refrigerator from the backup source. Cook a normal meal without the stove. Find the radio frequency. Recharge the lights. Discover the weak point while nothing is wrong.
