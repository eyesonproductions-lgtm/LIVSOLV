Preparedness is a spectrum

Start where you are. Get more capable from there.

You do not have to be a prepper to be prepared, and you do not have to buy everything at once. Cover the failures that would hurt your household most, test what you have, then add redundancy where it earns its place.

No bunker required. A little planning goes a long way.
01

Cover the first day

Safe light, drinking water, medications, phone power, food protection and reliable information. A surprising amount of this may already be in your house.

02

Get through a few days

Add safe cooking, first aid, communications, temperature planning and enough backup power for the loads that genuinely matter.

03

Add breathing room

Increase food and water reserves, add dependable recharging, strengthen communications and build household-specific plans.

04

Build real redundancy

Longer-duration energy, meaningful solar recharging, fuel options, evacuation planning and multiple ways to communicate when normal systems fail.

Think in failures, not disaster labels.

A hurricane, tornado, wildfire, ice storm, heat wave, equipment failure, grid emergency or planned shutoff can produce the same household problems: no electricity, unsafe temperatures, spoiled food, limited water and lost communications. Build capabilities that work across causes.

Test the system you think you have.

A battery specification, generator wattage or box of emergency food is not the same as a working household plan. Run the refrigerator. Cook a normal meal without the stove. Find the radio frequency. Recharge the lights. Discover the weak point while nothing is wrong.

There is no preparedness purity test.

A few days of supplies can be a meaningful improvement. So can a sophisticated solar and generator system. LivSolv respects the expertise of people who go deep into preparedness while making that knowledge usable for households that simply want to be less vulnerable.
