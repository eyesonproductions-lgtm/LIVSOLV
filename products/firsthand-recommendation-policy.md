# LivSolv Firsthand Product Recommendation Standard

Status: working editorial policy, September 2026

## Why this exists
LivSolv uses researched recommendations across many products, but the founder also owns and uses preparedness equipment in real life. Those are different evidence categories and should be labeled differently.

## The LivSolv decision-shortlist standard
LivSolv exists to do the comparison work before the reader arrives. A recommendation page should not recreate a retailer category page or hand someone a list of ten technically acceptable products.

For a normal buying decision, research the broader market privately, then surface **one or two winners** whenever the evidence supports a clean choice:

1. **Best value / lower-cost pick** — the least expensive product LivSolv can confidently recommend for the actual job. This is not automatically the absolute cheapest product on the market.
2. **Best overall pick** — the product LivSolv would choose when the reader is willing to spend more for meaningful reliability, capability, durability or ease of use.

A third product should appear only when it solves a genuinely different use case that the first two do not. Never create extra tiers just to fill space or increase affiliate-link count.

Every surfaced recommendation must answer, quickly: **Which one should I buy? Why this one? What do I give up by spending less? What do I gain by spending more?**

Where relevant, put the **$0 / use-what-you-have option** before the purchase choices. The goal is not to maximize the number of products shown. The goal is to minimize the reader's decision work without hiding an important tradeoff.

Purchase buttons should go to the exact recommended product or a clearly purchasable listing whenever possible. Do not send a reader to a manufacturer's broad catalog, corporate sales page or category page and make them search again unless no stable direct purchase destination exists.

## Preferred label
**LivSolv Firsthand Pick**

Supporting line when appropriate:
**Owned and used by LivSolv's founder.**

This is preferable to “author tested” because “tested” can imply a controlled comparative test protocol. Use “personally tested” only when we actually performed and can describe a meaningful test.

## Evidence labels
1. **LivSolv Firsthand Pick** — founder owns/has owned and has enough real use to recommend the exact make/model.
2. **LivSolv Long-Term Use** — founder has used the exact product over an extended period and can describe durability, maintenance and limitations.
3. **LivSolv Research Pick** — recommendation based on current specifications, independent evidence, practical sources and comparison research, without claiming ownership.
4. **Worth considering** — promising option where evidence is useful but not strong enough for a stronger endorsement.

## Rules for firsthand claims
- Confirm exact make/model before publishing the badge.
- Record approximate purchase date and use period if known.
- Record what was actually done with it: outage use, camping/RV use, charging, filtering, storage, etc.
- Record what the founder likes and dislikes. Firsthand does not mean uncritical.
- Separate subjective experience from measurable specifications.
- Re-check current model/specifications because manufacturers revise products.
- If the owned version is discontinued, say so and research the current replacement instead of silently treating it as identical.
- Never convert “I own one” into “best overall” without comparison research.
- Affiliate commission never changes the evidence label.

## Candidate founder-owned products to identify later
Do not publish exact recommendations until model identification is confirmed.
- Dual-fuel, RV-ready portable generator.
- Solar-rechargeable flashlights kept near windows for routine top-up. Need exact model and manufacturer charging/storage guidance before recommending that habit generally.
- C. Crane emergency radio. Need exact model. Possible models include the older CC Solar Observer and newer CCRadio Solar, but do not assume.
- Amateur/ham radio. Need exact model. Founder currently does not know how to operate it and is not licensed, so this is NOT currently a firsthand recommendation. It is instead a useful example of why owning communications equipment without training/licensing may not create usable capability.
- Wound-closure strips/device previously described as a zip-style closure. Need exact brand/model before firsthand badge.
- Water filters/purifiers founder recommends. Need exact make/model and actual use history.

## Solar-in-window caution
Do not make a universal claim that leaving every solar flashlight in a window continuously is recommended. Solar charging performance and battery/heat guidance vary by model. Some solar emergency products are explicitly designed to stay topped up in a sunny window, while other products may charge poorly behind coated glass or may have battery-temperature/storage constraints. Verify the exact flashlight manual before publishing the practice as advice.

## Ham-radio accuracy
For U.S. amateur radio, normal transmitting requires the appropriate FCC amateur license/control operator. Listening/receiving is different from transmitting. Emergency exceptions are narrow and must not be marketed as a substitute for licensing and learning the equipment in advance. LivSolv should emphasize: equipment you cannot legally/practically operate before an emergency is not a complete communications plan.

### [Product name]

What we'd change: [honest limitation]

Best for: [use case]

Research check: [current specifications / independent evidence]
