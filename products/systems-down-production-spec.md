# Systems Down: First 72 Hours
## Digital Production Specification

Status: production direction approved  
Last updated: 2026-09-15

## Product promise

In one focused session, a household identifies what must keep working, what can wait, where its real gaps are, and the next three purchases or actions that provide the most useful protection.

This is not a giant record-keeping binder. It is a guided decision tool.

## Customer package

Deliver one ZIP file containing:

1. `START-HERE.pdf`
2. `Systems-Down-Audit-US-Letter.pdf`
3. `Systems-Down-Audit-A4.pdf`
4. `Systems-Down-Audit-Ink-Saver-US-Letter.pdf`
5. `Systems-Down-Audit-Ink-Saver-A4.pdf`
6. `Systems-Down-Calculator.xlsx`
7. `Systems-Down-Quick-Cards.pdf`
8. `READ-ME.txt`

The two primary audit PDFs should be fillable on a computer and printable. Do not require Canva, special fonts, a paid app, or a customer account.

## Core audit length

Target: 12 to 15 pages, including cover and final action plan.

1. Cover and promise
2. How to use this in 20 minutes
3. Household snapshot
4. The non-negotiables: people, health, pets, temperature, and location
5. What must stay powered
6. Communications and information
7. Water and food reality check
8. Heating, cooling, and safe shelter
9. Medical, mobility, and sensory needs
10. Home, vehicle, and evacuation constraints
11. Current equipment and honest runtime
12. Gap finder: covered, fragile, missing, or irrelevant
13. Priority plan: do now, buy next, skip for now
14. First 72 hours one-page plan
15. Review date and change log

Combine pages when testing shows the same decision can be made without extra friction.

## Decision logic

Every section must produce an outcome, not merely collect information.

Use four clear states:

- Covered
- Fragile
- Missing
- Not relevant

Each identified gap must end with one of:

- No-cost action
- Low-cost improvement
- Larger purchase to research
- Deliberate decision to accept the risk

The final plan must limit the customer to:

- Three actions this week
- Three items to research or buy next
- Three things they do not need to buy now

## Calculator

The spreadsheet supports the audit. It does not lead the experience.

Required outputs:

- Essential device or appliance
- Running watts
- Estimated startup watts when relevant
- Hours needed per day
- Daily watt-hours
- Recommended reserve margin
- Estimated runtime for a user-entered battery capacity
- Plain-language warning when an estimate depends on missing or uncertain inputs

Protect formula cells, distinguish inputs from outputs, and include one completed example.

## Quick cards

Create compact print-and-cut cards for:

- Household contacts and meeting point
- Medical and pet essentials
- Utility shutoffs and key locations
- Power priorities
- First 24-hour actions

Do not put full passwords, Social Security numbers, complete financial account numbers, or other high-risk data on the cards.

## Design requirements

- Calm and editorial, not tactical or fear-based
- LIVSOLV forest, ivory, sage, amber, and ink palette
- Strong hierarchy and generous writing space
- Minimum 11-point body text where practical
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate status
- Form fields must have visible labels and sensible tab order
- Actual photography only when needed; a photo-free product is acceptable
- No filler pages added to inflate page count

## Quality checks

Before release:

- Test every form field in Adobe Acrobat Reader and a browser PDF viewer.
- Print every version at 100 percent on US Letter and A4.
- Confirm ink-saver pages remain understandable in grayscale.
- Recalculate every spreadsheet example manually.
- Test the entire audit with at least three different household scenarios.
- Confirm ZIP opens on macOS, Windows, iPhone, and Android.
- Proof all safety language and clearly label estimates.
- Verify filenames and page numbers match the Start Here guide.
