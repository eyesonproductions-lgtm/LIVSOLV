SCREEN-FREE BACKUP PLAN

When the Wi-Fi dies, boredom arrives about six minutes later.

Keeping everyone occupied is not the most serious part of an outage, but it matters. A few genuinely fun games can turn a dark, stressful evening into family time instead of four people staring at dead phones.

Games worth keeping in the outage box

We favor games that need no power, are easy to learn, work across ages and are entertaining enough that adults actually want to play too.

OWNED + LOVED
BS, also called Bluff or I Doubt It

This is one of our family's favorites. It uses an ordinary deck of cards and the entire game is basically trying to bluff the people who know you best. Our family regularly ends up in tears laughing at each other. Best part: there is nothing special to buy.

For little kids: build a quiet-time envelope

Keep coloring books or printed coloring pages, crayons, blank paper, stickers and a pencil pouch together. During a stressful outage, having a familiar quiet activity ready is much easier than inventing entertainment on the spot. For younger children, giant paper or a roll of paper can turn coloring into a much longer group activity.

Preparedness is also keeping people okay.

Food, water, light and communication come first. But comfort and distraction matter too, especially for children who may already know something unusual is happening. You do not need a closet full of toys. A deck of cards, two excellent games and a quiet activity can cover a surprising amount of time.
