How do I still get information and reach people?

You do not need every kind of radio. Start with a free family plan, then add only the communication layers your household actually needs.

Do this for free today: choose one meeting place near home and one outside your neighborhood, choose one out-of-area contact, create a family group text, turn on emergency alerts, and put the important numbers and meeting places on paper.

Why these free steps matter

During a regional emergency, calls may fail or networks may be overloaded. Texts can sometimes get through when calls do not. A paper plan still works if a phone is dead, lost or inaccessible. Everyone should know where to meet and who the out-of-area contact is before an emergency starts.

2. Talk to family or neighbors nearby

Rocky Talkie Mountain Radio

You are talking directly to another person with a compatible FRS radio, such as a partner, child, neighbor or another car in your group. You generally need at least two radios unless the other person already owns one.

This does not automatically reach 911. Terrain, hills, trees and buildings can dramatically reduce range, so test the places you care about.

4. Want ham capability?

Owned

TIDRADIO TD-H3

This is the compact starter radio we own and are currently learning. It is a 5W handheld with USB-C charging/programming, Bluetooth phone programming, repeater capability, NOAA weather reception, scanning and a 2500mAh rechargeable battery. It is inexpensive enough to learn on without committing to a big radio setup.

You can own it and listen without an amateur-radio license. In normal U.S. use, transmitting on amateur frequencies requires the appropriate FCC amateur license. Learn the controls, program local frequencies and repeaters, and practice legally before you ever need it in an emergency.

Important: TD-H3 is sold in Ham, GMRS and unlocked configurations. What you are legally allowed to transmit on depends on the version and service. Do not assume an unlocked frequency means you are authorized to transmit there.

5. Keep your phone useful without service

Download offline maps before a storm or trip. Save critical addresses, contacts, medical information and meeting locations locally. GPS can still determine your position without cellular data, but the map itself needs to already be stored on the device.

Easy memory:
NOAA radio = hear what is happening.
FRS radios = talk to your own people nearby.
Satellite messenger = reach people beyond the local outage.
Ham = powerful, but requires learning.
Offline phone + paper = navigation and information when networks disappear.

Before you put the gear away

1. Charge it.
2. Activate anything requiring a subscription.
3. Program contacts and channels.
4. Test it where you expect to use it.
5. Put the instructions and charging cable with the device.
